Local to Aljezur

Painting & decorating in Aljezur

Aljezur is a laid-back town on the west coast, popular with owners drawn to the wilder Costa Vicentina coastline. Whether it's a villa, apartment, holiday rental or commercial premises, the work is quoted around the condition of the actual surfaces — and every step is explained in English.

Aljezur sits on the Costa Vicentina, the Algarve’s wilder Atlantic-facing coast, and property here divides fairly cleanly between the old town, rural properties on land outside it, and a smaller number of newer builds. The Atlantic exposure that defines Sagres applies here too, particularly on anything with a westward aspect, while the rural properties bring a different problem entirely: older, sometimes lime-based render on traditional construction, where the wrong modern coating traps moisture and does more harm than leaving it alone.

Painting in Aljezur

What to know before painting in Aljezur

Property Types & Profile

The stock here is more varied and more rural than the resort towns. The old town has traditional houses with painted render fronting narrow streets; outside it, properties sit on land — converted farmhouses, restored rural buildings and self-built homes — often with outbuildings, long boundary walls and a good deal of exposed timber and metalwork. Newer builds exist but they are a smaller share of the market than in Lagos or Portimão, and construction varies far more here than in a modern development.

What Owners Tend to Ask About

Two things come up most. First, exposure: a westward elevation on this coast weathers hard, and owners want to know what will actually hold rather than what looks good for two seasons. Second, older construction: on a traditional rural building, a modern impermeable coating over lime-based render traps moisture in the wall and pushes the paint off from behind, so what looks like a paint failure is really a specification failure. Owners restoring rural property here often arrive at that problem after someone has already painted it wrong once.

Questions

Frequently asked questions about Aljezur

My rural property has old lime render — can it just be painted normally?

It should not be. A modern impermeable coating over traditional lime-based render traps moisture in the wall, and it pushes the paint off from behind rather than wearing off the front. Breathable systems are the right answer on that construction, and identifying which you have comes before choosing anything.

Is Aljezur as exposed as Sagres?

Close, on anything facing west. The Atlantic exposure along the Costa Vicentina is the defining factor for coastal-facing property here, though land a little inland and in the valley is meaningfully more sheltered.

A previous paint job here failed within a couple of years — why?

On a rural property the most common cause is the wrong specification on traditional render — trapping moisture. On an exposed coastal one it is more often preparation, painting over a chalked or salted surface. Both are worth establishing before repeating the same approach.

Do you work on outbuildings and boundary walls as well?

Yes, and on rural properties here they often account for more wall area than the house. They are usually in worse condition too, because they get looked at last.

What about the timber on a restored rural building?

Older timber on a rural property is usually well worth saving — greying and surface splitting sand out, and only soft, rotten sections genuinely need replacing. See <a href="/wood-shutter-treatment">wood and shutter treatment</a>.

Is the work here harder to access than in the towns?

Often, yes. Rural properties on land can mean longer runs, uneven ground and no straightforward place to set up, and that affects a quote more than the size of the building does.

Can you tell what kind of render I have from a photo?

Sometimes, and it is worth sending one — but on a traditional building it usually needs looking at properly, because getting the breathability question wrong is the single most expensive mistake to make on that kind of wall.

Is spring or autumn better for exterior work here?

Both work, with autumn generally preferred if the goal is getting an exposed elevation sound before the winter storms rather than after them.
