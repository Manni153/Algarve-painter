Painting & decorating in Olhão

Olhão is a working fishing town on the eastern Algarve with a growing expat community. Whether it's a villa, apartment, holiday rental or commercial premises, the work is quoted around the condition of the actual surfaces — and every step is explained in English.

Olhão has an architecture that genuinely changes the work: the old town’s cube houses, with their flat roof terraces and stepped, angular massing, mean that flat-roof waterproofing is not an optional extra here but often the central job. Combine that with older construction, a working town centre rather than a resort one, and a growing number of restoration projects among incoming owners, and the typical job here looks quite different from a modern villa on the coast.

What to know before painting in Olhão

Property Types & Profile

The old town is dense and distinctive — flat-roofed cube houses with roof terraces and açoteias, narrow streets, and traditional render over older construction. Many are being restored by incoming owners, with varying quality of previous work underneath. Beyond the old town there are apartments and more modern housing, and the waterfront and market area anchor a working commercial centre. The Ria Formosa alongside means some salt exposure, though sheltered compared with the open coast.

What Owners Tend to Ask About

Water gets raised more here than in most towns, and reasonably so: a flat roof terrace has far more detailing to fail than a pitched roof, and on older construction those details have often been patched repeatedly. Owners restoring old-town property also frequently discover previous work done in the wrong materials — impermeable coatings on traditional render, or repairs that move differently from the wall and keep cracking. The narrow streets make access a planning matter as well.

Questions

Frequently asked questions about Olhão

My house has a flat roof terrace — what needs checking?

The details rather than the open area: the upstands where the roof turns up into the parapet, the outlets, any penetrations, and the door threshold onto the terrace. Those are where flat roofs actually fail — see <a href="/waterproof-roof-coating">waterproof and roof coating</a>.

Water is coming in but I cannot find where from — is that normal?

Very. Water tracks along the underside of a slab and emerges at the weakest point it reaches, often metres from where it entered. Finding the entry point is a separate step from seeing the damage.

Can old-town render be painted with normal masonry paint?

It should be checked first. Traditional render needs a breathable coating, and an impermeable modern paint traps moisture in the wall and pushes the film off from behind.

I have bought a house to restore — where should I start?

With whatever is letting water in — roof terrace details and cracked render — before any decorating. Doing the water-related work first is consistently cheaper across five years than starting with what looks worst.

Previous repairs on my house are cracking around the edges — why?

They were made in a material that moves differently from the surrounding render, so they have separated at the joint. Those usually need cutting out and redoing rather than filling the outline again.

Are the narrow old-town streets a problem for access?

They need planning rather than being a problem. Working on a frontage onto a narrow public street means agreeing access and how to work around pedestrians before starting.

Does being on the Ria Formosa mean salt problems?

Some, but well short of an open-coast town. Metalwork corrodes here, just more slowly than it would on an exposed seafront.

Can the roof terrace still be used after it is coated?

Yes, provided it is specified for foot traffic from the outset. A surface intended for a roof nobody walks on will not stand up to furniture and daily use, so that needs saying up front.
