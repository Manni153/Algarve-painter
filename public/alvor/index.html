Local to Alvor

Painting & decorating in Alvor

Alvor is a small fishing town turned popular residential and holiday destination. Whether it's a villa, apartment, holiday rental or commercial premises, the work is quoted around the condition of the actual surfaces — and every step is explained in English.

Alvor keeps the shape of the fishing town it was — a compact old centre of low whitewashed houses and narrow streets — with residential and holiday development spreading out around it toward the estuary and the beach. The whitewashed look that defines the centre is also what drives the work: bright white render shows soiling, algae and salt bloom far more readily than a coloured wall, so properties here tend to look tired sooner than they actually are, and a wash-down often reveals a wall that does not need repainting yet.

Painting in Alvor

What to know before painting in Alvor

Property Types & Profile

The old centre is dense, low-rise and traditionally whitewashed, with houses fronting directly onto narrow streets and small terraces or roof terraces rather than gardens. Surrounding that are villas, townhouses and apartment developments on more generous plots, many built or bought for holiday use and let through the season. Proximity to the estuary and the beach means salt exposure is a real factor across the town, and roof terraces are common enough that flat-roof detailing comes up more here than in towns with predominantly pitched roofs.

What Owners Tend to Ask About

Owners here mostly want to know two things: whether the walls genuinely need repainting or just cleaning, and how to fit the work into a calendar dominated by the letting season. Bright white render soils quickly and grows algae on shaded elevations, which reads as needing paint when it often needs washing. And with much of the stock let through summer, the practical window for anything disruptive is narrow, so scope and sequence matter more than they would on a permanently occupied home.

Questions

Frequently asked questions about Alvor

The white walls look grey — do they need repainting?

Often not. Bright white render shows dust, traffic film and algae far more than a coloured wall does, and washing a test area is the quickest way to find out. If it comes up clean, the property may have another season or two in it.

Why does green or black growth keep coming back on one wall?

That wall is not getting enough sun to dry out. It needs treating and killing rather than just pressure washing — washing removes what you can see and leaves what brings it back within a season.

Can work be fitted around the letting season?

Yes, and here it usually has to be. Most work on let properties in Alvor happens in an off-season window or a changeover gap, with the scope set to what genuinely fits rather than overrunning into a booking.

Do the narrow old-town streets make access difficult?

They make it different rather than difficult. Working on a frontage directly onto a public street needs planning around access and pedestrians, and that gets agreed before anything starts.

A lot of properties here have roof terraces — is that relevant?

Very. Flat roofs and roof terraces are where most water ingress starts, and the details — upstands, outlets, thresholds — fail long before the open area does. See <a href="/waterproof-roof-coating">waterproof and roof coating</a>.

Does the estuary and beach proximity affect the property?

Mainly through salt on metalwork. Railings, grilles and gates here corrode faster than the same metal further inland, and that usually needs attention before the walls do.

Should the whole old-town frontage be done at once?

On a narrow-street frontage, yes — it is a single visible surface, and doing part of it leaves a visible line. It is also the elevation the property is judged on from the street.

Is it worth painting the roof terrace walls at the same time?

Usually. Access is already set up, and terrace walls take more sun and weather than the elevations below them, so they tend to be in worse condition than owners expect.
