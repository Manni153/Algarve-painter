What our Exterior House Painting service covers

Exterior painting is usually the first thing an owner looks at when a property starts to feel tired — chalky, faded walls, hairline cracks tracking out from window corners, or one elevation that has clearly aged faster than the rest.

  • Full wash-down to remove dust, salt film, pollen and surface growth before anything else
  • Scraping back and removing loose, chalking or flaking previous coatings
  • Raking out and filling cracks, and making good damaged render before painting
  • Priming bare, repaired and previously untreated patches so the topcoat binds evenly
  • Masking and sheeting of windows, shutters, terraces, pool surrounds and planting
  • Breathable, UV-stable exterior coatings suited to Algarve sun and coastal salt air
  • Villas, townhouses, apartment blocks and commercial frontages
Why It Matters

Why this matters on an Algarve property

Exterior paint on an Algarve property is doing more than making it look tidy — it is the layer taking the direct hit from summer sun, salt-laden air and winter rain, and it is what keeps water out of the render behind it. Letting it go too long turns a straightforward repaint into render repair, which costs more and takes longer. For owners who are not on-site year-round, a repaint on a sensible cycle is also the difference between arriving to a property that looks looked-after and one that looks abandoned.

In Depth

How exterior house painting actually works

Why Preparation Decides How Long the Paint Lasts

The single biggest difference between an exterior that still looks good in five years and one that is flaking after two is what happened before the first topcoat. On an Algarve property that usually means washing off years of accumulated dust, pollen and salt film, scraping back anything that has already lost its grip, raking out and filling cracks properly rather than skimming over them, treating any salt bloom or damp staining at source, and priming every bare or repaired patch so the topcoat has a consistent surface to bind to. Skipping those steps makes a quote look cheaper and makes the result fail early — new paint bonds to whatever is under it, so painting over a chalky or loose surface simply buys a couple of seasons before the whole thing lifts.

Why Two Quotes for Similar-Looking Villas Can Differ

The most common reason two exterior quotes for what look like comparable properties come out differently is access and condition, not floor area. A single-storey villa where every wall can be reached from a ladder or a light tower is a more contained job than a three-storey townhouse on a sloping plot needing scaffold, or a property where the pool terrace has to be fully sheeted before anyone starts. Condition matters just as much: a wall that needs nothing more than a wash and two coats is a completely different job from one carrying widespread cracking, previous patch repairs in the wrong material, or a coating that has to come off before anything new goes on. That is why an accurate number comes from looking at the property rather than a rate per square metre.

Real-World Scenarios

Where exterior house painting actually gets used

01

The South or West Elevation That Aged First

One wall taking direct afternoon sun and driven rain almost always fails before the others. Repainting just that elevation is often the right call, with the caveat that fresh paint will read brighter than the faded walls next to it until they weather in.

02

Getting a Property Ready to Sell or Let

A repainted exterior changes the first impression a property makes in photographs and at a viewing more than almost any other single job at the same cost — and it is generally the last work you want happening while a property is already on the market.

03

A Villa Bought With Years of Deferred Maintenance

Newly bought properties often come with a decade of postponed exterior work. Assessing what genuinely needs doing now versus what will hold another season keeps the first year of ownership from turning into one continuous building site.

04

Apartment Block or Condominium Frontage

Shared buildings involve a committee, a budget and a schedule rather than a single owner making a call. Work is planned around residents and access to shared entrances, with the sequencing agreed before anything starts.

Common Problems

What owners actually call about

The specific issues that come up most, and what's usually behind them.

“Paint is chalky and comes off on your hand when you touch the wall”

Normal UV breakdown of the binder in an older coating, accelerated on south and west elevations. It has to be washed and scraped back to a sound surface and primed — painting straight over chalk means the new coat bonds to loose powder rather than to the wall.

“Hairline cracks tracking diagonally out from window and door corners”

Extremely common and usually cosmetic — stress cracking in the render at the weakest point of the opening rather than structural movement. They get raked out, filled and reinforced before painting. Cracks that are wide, stepped, or that keep reopening in the same place after repair are a different conversation and worth having before repainting over them.

“White crystalline powder or staining coming through the paint low down on a wall”

Salt bloom — moisture moving through the wall and depositing salts as it evaporates. Painting over it traps the moisture and it will come back through. The source of the damp has to be dealt with and the wall allowed to dry before a breathable coating goes on.

“Black or green growth on a shaded, north-facing wall”

Algae and mould thriving where a wall never dries out fully. It gets treated and killed rather than just jet-washed off, because pressure washing alone removes what you can see and leaves the spores that bring it back within a season.

“Paint bubbling and lifting away in sheets rather than flaking”

Almost always moisture behind the coating, or a previous non-breathable paint trapping it. The affected area has to come back to a sound surface and the cause found first — recoating over it repeats the same failure in the same place.

“A repaired patch is a visibly different shade and texture from the wall around it”

Filler and new render absorb differently from aged, weathered paint, so a patch shows through even in a matched colour. Priming the repair and painting the full elevation rather than the patch is what actually makes it disappear.

“A previous repaint has already failed after two or three years”

Usually a preparation shortcut rather than a bad product — painted over chalk, over damp, or without priming bare patches. Worth establishing what was skipped before repeating the same specification and expecting a different outcome.

“Rust stains bleeding down the wall from railings or a balcony”

The metalwork is corroding and the runoff is staining the render. Painting the wall alone leaves the source in place and the staining returns — the metal needs treating at the same time.

“The render sounds hollow when tapped in places”

The render has debonded from the substrate underneath. That area needs cutting out and making good before painting, because a coating over hollow render simply comes away with it when it eventually lets go.

“Colour has faded unevenly across the same wall”

Usually shading from a tree, a balcony overhang or a neighbouring building giving part of the wall meaningfully less UV exposure. Not a fault, and not fixable by touching up — the elevation reads as one surface only once it is painted as one.

“Nobody has painted the property since it was bought and it is not clear where to start”

Start with a walk round and a written order of priority: which elevations are actually failing, which are cosmetic, and what is letting water in. Doing the water-related work first and the cosmetic work after is almost always cheaper over five years than doing whatever looks worst.

In Detail

Exterior House Painting, done properly

Washing Down and Cutting Back

Every exterior starts with a wash to remove the film of dust, salt and organic growth that builds up on Algarve properties, followed by scraping and sanding back anything that is chalking, flaking or already lifting. It is the least visible part of the job and the part that decides how long the result holds.

Crack Repair and Making Good

Cracks are raked out and filled rather than skimmed over, damaged or hollow render is cut back and made good, and repairs are primed before painting. Where the cracking is widespread, that becomes a job in its own right — see render and crack repair.

Coatings Chosen for the Climate

Strong UV, salt air near the coast and heavy winter rain all shorten the life of an exterior coating. Breathable, UV-stable products are specified for that combination, and the specification is matched to the wall in front of us rather than applied identically to every property.

Protecting Everything That Is Not Being Painted

Windows, shutters, ironwork, terraces, pool surrounds, outdoor furniture and planting are masked and sheeted before work starts, and the site is cleared at the end of each day rather than left as it stands until the job finishes.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to paint the outside of a villa?

It depends on the size of the property, the condition of the existing surfaces, how much crack repair and preparation is involved and whether scaffold or access equipment is needed — there is no single price that applies to every property. The most accurate way to get a number is a quick call or WhatsApp message describing the property.

How long does an exterior repaint take?

It depends on the size of the property and how much preparation the surfaces need — a villa that only needs washing and two coats is a more contained job than one carrying widespread cracking or a failed previous coating. Timing is confirmed as part of the survey, not before it.

What time of year is best for exterior painting here?

Spring and autumn are generally the most forgiving. In high summer a south-facing wall can get hot enough that paint flashes off before it levels properly, and in midwinter overnight damp and rain stop a coat curing — so the order of elevations gets planned around the weather and which side is in shade.

Do the walls need to be repaired before painting?

Where there is cracking, hollow render or damage, yes — see render and crack repair. Painting over unrepaired render hides the problem for a season rather than solving it, and the coating fails again in the same places.

Can you paint just one elevation rather than the whole property?

Yes, and it is often the sensible call when one wall has aged much faster than the others. The one thing to expect is that the freshly painted side will read brighter than the faded walls beside it until they weather in.

Will you paint the shutters and railings at the same time?

They are usually done as part of the same visit — see wood and shutter treatment and metalwork and railing painting. Doing them together avoids paying twice for access to the same walls.

Do I need to be at the property while the work happens?

No. For second homes and rentals this is routine — access is arranged with whoever holds keys locally, progress photos are sent through as the work goes, and the final walkthrough can happen over a video call.

How often should an Algarve exterior be repainted?

It varies more by elevation and exposure than by a fixed number of years — a sheltered north wall holds far longer than a south or west wall taking direct sun and salt air. Rather than a schedule, the useful signal is the paint starting to chalk when you rub it, which means the coating is breaking down and water is getting closer to the render.

Can you match the colour the property already is?

In most cases yes, matched closely from a sample. Worth knowing that a matched colour will still look newer than the weathered paint next to it, so matching works best for a repair or a full elevation rather than a patch in the middle of a wall.

Do you paint boundary walls and garden walls too?

Yes — boundary walls, garden walls, gate pillars and outbuildings are usually quoted alongside the house itself, since they are what makes a property read as finished rather than half done from the road.

Can you work on an apartment block or a shared building?

Yes. Shared buildings involve the condominium and its budget rather than one owner, so the scope, sequencing and access to common areas get agreed up front and the work is planned around residents.

What happens if it rains partway through the job?

Work pauses on the affected elevation and the surface is allowed to dry before anything else goes on, rather than being coated over damp. In practice that means the running order shifts to a sheltered side of the property while the weather passes.
